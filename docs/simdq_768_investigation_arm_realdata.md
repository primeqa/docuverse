Reading it:

- **Single-thread (t=1)**: NEON is **1.13× faster than AVX2** at 384d (35.8 vs 40.4 ms) and **1.70× faster** at 768d (77.1 vs 131.3 ms). It sits between AVX2 and AVX-512 despite the register being half AVX2's width. The reason is that AVX2's b=2 unpack goes through a stack roundtrip (`vf[8]` → `_mm256_loadu_ps`); NEON has the same shape but a shorter dependency chain per block, and Apple's firestorm cores have 4× FP32 FMA units. AVX-512 wins on architectures with the VBMI `vpmultishiftqb` in-register unpack (~4.5× over the roundtrip).
- **All-threads**: AVX-512+BLAS-pinned wins at 768d (6.3 ms). AVX2 pulls ahead at 384d (2.77 vs NEON's 6.17). NEON's ceiling is set by having 8 P-cores (not 16), so it can't quite reach the AVX2/AVX-512 saturation.
- **NEON sweet spot is t=8** (best perf both dims). t=10/t=all adds the 2 E-cores which are ~2× slower than P-cores and drag the median up. Users on M1/M2/M3 Max should pass `num_threads=8`, not `num_threads=0`.

Reading it:

- Hamming is **memory-bound**, so the ISA difference is small: NEON's native `vcntq_u8` (single-cycle) matches AVX2's LUT-based popcount in throughput. At t=1 NEON and AVX2 are **within 1%** at both dims.
- At t=all NEON is ~15-25% behind AVX2, again limited by 8 P-cores vs 16. The E-core regression is smaller than for asym b=2 because the workload is bandwidth-bound and the E-cores have their own L2.
- AVX-512's Phase 8 SoA-storage fix (0.39 ms/q on 768d 16-conc) is the reference point for what fully-parallel Hamming can do; per-thread it's the same as AVX2/NEON.

### asym-b2 vs Hamming ratio

| arch | 384d | 768d |
|---|---|---|
| AVX-512 (VBMI, from Phase 8) | 0.41 / 1.14 = **0.36×** (b=2 is *faster*) | 0.81 / 3.5 was pre-SoA-fix; post-fix Hamming 0.39, b=2 0.81 → **2.1×** |
| AVX2 (5955WX, 16-conc)       | 2.89 / 0.10 = **27.5×** | 8.03 / 0.17 = **47.3×** |
| **NEON (M1 Max, t=all)**     | 7.40 / 0.35 = **21×**   | 15.46 / 0.61 = **25×** |

The b=2 / Hamming ratio is a rough proxy for how much the b=2 unpack costs vs a native popcount. On AVX-512 with VBMI in-register unpack it's close to 1×. On AVX2 the scalar-array roundtrip is expensive, so Hamming looks disproportionately fast (~40×). On NEON we sit in between (~20-25×) — the unpack is still scalar+load, but NEON's popcount doesn't get the extra "LUT is cheap, unpack is dear" AVX2 asymmetry.

NEON on M1 Max is competitive on Hamming (within 15-40%), and on asym b=2 is roughly half the throughput of a 16-core x86 — expected given 8 P-cores vs 16 x86 cores and 128-bit vs 256/512-bit vectors. Per-core the NEON kernel is **stronger** than AVX2 (35.8 vs 40.4 ms at 384d single-thread), just outnumbered. On workloads that pin `num_threads=8`, an M-series laptop is a first-class simdq target for dev and moderate corpora.
